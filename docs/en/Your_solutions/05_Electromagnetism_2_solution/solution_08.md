## 8. EM Spectrum

List the following types of electromagnetic radiation in order of increasing wavelength: Infrared, Ultraviolet, Microwaves, X-rays, Radio waves, Gamma rays.

---

### Variables and Units

While this specific problem does not require calculating numerical values, understanding the underlying variables is key to solving it:

*   **$\lambda$ (lambda):** Wavelength. The physical distance over which the wave's shape repeats. It is measured in **meters (m)**, though for the EM spectrum, we often use smaller units like nanometers ($10^{-9}$ m) or picometers ($10^{-12}$ m).
*   **$f$ (or $\nu$):** Frequency. The number of wave cycles passing a point per second, measured in **Hertz (Hz)**.
*   **$E$:** Photon Energy. The energy carried by a single photon of the radiation, typically measured in **Electron-volts (eV)** or **Joules (J)**.

*Key Concept:* Wavelength is inversely proportional to both frequency and energy. High-energy radiation has a very short wavelength, while low-energy radiation has a very long wavelength.

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Goal:** Order the following from shortest wavelength to longest wavelength: *Infrared, Ultraviolet, Microwaves, X-rays, Radio waves, Gamma rays.*

**Step 1: Identify the shortest wavelength (highest energy)**
Gamma rays are produced by nuclear transitions and cosmic events. They have the highest energy, highest frequency, and therefore the shortest wavelength in the electromagnetic spectrum (often less than $10^{-11}$ meters).
*   *Current Order:* Gamma rays

**Step 2: Identify the next shortest wavelength**
X-rays have slightly less energy than gamma rays (often overlapping slightly depending on the source). They are commonly used in medical imaging because their short wavelengths (around $10^{-11}$ to $10^{-8}$ meters) allow them to pass through soft tissue.
*   *Current Order:* Gamma rays < X-rays

**Step 3: Move towards the visible spectrum**
Ultraviolet (UV) radiation sits just beyond the violet end of the visible light spectrum. It has less energy than X-rays but more than visible light, with wavelengths ranging from about $10 \text{ nm}$ to $400 \text{ nm}$.
*   *Current Order:* Gamma rays < X-rays < Ultraviolet

**Step 4: Cross the visible spectrum to lower energies**
Infrared (IR) radiation sits just beyond the red end of the visible spectrum. We often perceive it as heat. Its wavelengths are longer than visible light, ranging from about $700 \text{ nm}$ up to $1 \text{ mm}$.
*   *Current Order:* Gamma rays < X-rays < Ultraviolet < Infrared

**Step 5: Identify the longer wavelengths**
Microwaves have longer wavelengths than infrared. They are used in radar, communications, and cooking, with wavelengths ranging from about $1 \text{ mm}$ to $1 \text{ meter}$.
*   *Current Order:* Gamma rays < X-rays < Ultraviolet < Infrared < Microwaves

**Step 6: Identify the longest wavelength (lowest energy)**
Radio waves are used for long-distance communication and broadcasting. They have the lowest frequency, lowest energy, and the longest wavelengths, ranging from $1 \text{ meter}$ to hundreds of kilometers.
*   *Final Order:* Gamma rays < X-rays < Ultraviolet < Infrared < Microwaves < Radio waves
