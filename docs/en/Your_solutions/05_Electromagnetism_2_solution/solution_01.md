## 1. Gauss's Law

A point charge of $+2 \text{ C}$ is located at the origin. Calculate the electric flux through a spherical surface of radius $1 \text{ m}$ centered at the origin.

---

### **Variables and Units Explained**

*   **$\Phi_E$ (Electric Flux):** Represents the measure of the "flow" of the electric field through a given area. Measured in Newton square meters per Coulomb (N·m²/C) or Volt meters (V·m).
*   **$q_{\text{enc}}$ (Enclosed Charge):** The total net electric charge contained within the closed surface. Measured in Coulombs (C).
*   **$\varepsilon_0$ (Vacuum Permittivity):** A fundamental physical constant representing the capability of a vacuum to permit electric field lines. Measured in Farads per meter (F/m), which is equivalent to C²/(N·m²).
*   **$r$ (Radius):** The distance from the center of the sphere to its surface. Measured in meters (m).

---

### **Solution: Calculating Electric Flux**

**Step 1: State the relevant physics principle (Gauss's Law)**
Gauss's Law states that the net electric flux through any closed hypothetical surface (a Gaussian surface) is equal to the net electric charge enclosed within that closed surface divided by the vacuum permittivity. 

The formula is:
$$\Phi_E = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$$

**Step 2: Identify the given values and constants**
*   Enclosed charge ($q_{\text{enc}}$) = 2 C
*   Radius of the sphere ($r$) = 1 m 
*   Vacuum permittivity constant ($\varepsilon_0$) $\approx$ 8.854 × 10⁻¹² F/m

*Note: According to Gauss's Law, the total electric flux depends solely on the enclosed charge. The size or shape of the enclosing surface does not matter. Therefore, the radius of 1 m is extra information and will not change the final calculation.*

**Step 3: Perform the calculation**
Substitute the enclosed charge and the permittivity constant into the formula:
$$\Phi_E = \frac{2}{8.854 \times 10^{-12}}$$

$$\Phi_E \approx 2.259 \times 10^{11}$$

The electric flux through the spherical surface is approximately **2.259 × 10¹¹ N·m²/C**.
