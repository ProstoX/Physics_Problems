## 4. Magnetic Torque

A rectangular loop of wire with dimensions $10 \text{ cm}$ by $5 \text{ cm}$ carries a current of $2 \text{ A}$. A uniform magnetic field of $B = 0.3 \text{ T}$ is applied parallel to the plane of the loop. What is the magnitude of the magnetic torque on the loop?

---

### **Variables and Units Explained**

*   **$\tau$ (Magnetic Torque):** Represents the rotational force experienced by the loop in the magnetic field. Measured in Newton-meters ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$).
*   **$N$ (Number of Turns):** The number of times the wire is coiled. For a single loop, $N = 1$. It is a dimensionless quantity.
*   **$I$ (Current):** The electric current flowing through the rectangular loop. Measured in Amperes (A).
*   **$A$ (Area):** The cross-sectional area enclosed by the wire loop. Measured in square meters ($\text{m}^2$).
*   **$B$ (Magnetic Field):** The strength of the uniform magnetic field in which the loop is placed. Measured in Teslas (T).
*   **$\theta$ (Angle):** The angle between the area vector (a line pointing straight out, perpendicular to the plane of the loop) and the magnetic field lines. Measured in degrees ($^\circ$) or radians.

---

### **Solution: Calculating Magnetic Torque**

**Step 1: Identify given information and convert to SI units**
*   Length of loop ($L$) = $10 \text{ cm} = 0.1 \text{ m}$
*   Width of loop ($W$) = $5 \text{ cm} = 0.05 \text{ m}$
*   Current ($I$) = $2 \text{ A}$
*   Magnetic field ($B$) = $0.3 \text{ T}$
*   Number of turns ($N$) = $1$ (since it is a single "loop")

**Step 2: Calculate the area of the loop ($A$)**
The area of a rectangle is length multiplied by width.
$$A = L \times W$$
$$A = 0.1 \text{ m} \times 0.05 \text{ m} = 0.005 \text{ m}^2$$

**Step 3: Determine the angle ($\theta$)**
The problem states the magnetic field is applied *parallel to the plane of the loop*. 
The area vector is always *perpendicular* (normal) to the plane of the loop. 
Therefore, if the field is parallel to the plane, the angle $\theta$ between the area vector and the magnetic field vector is exactly $90^\circ$.

**Step 4: Apply the magnetic torque formula**
The magnitude of the torque on a current-carrying loop is given by:
$$\tau = N \cdot I \cdot A \cdot B \cdot \sin(\theta)$$

Substitute the known values into the equation:
$$\tau = 1 \cdot (2) \cdot (0.005) \cdot (0.3) \cdot \sin(90^\circ)$$

Since $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$:
$$\tau = 2 \cdot 0.005 \cdot 0.3 \cdot 1$$
$$\tau = 0.01 \cdot 0.3$$
$$\tau = 0.003 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

The magnitude of the magnetic torque on the loop is **$0.003 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$** (or $3.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$). This is the maximum possible torque for this loop, as it occurs when the plane is parallel to the field.
