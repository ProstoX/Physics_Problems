## 9. Refraction (Snell's Law)

A light ray travels from air ($n=1.00$) into glass ($n=1.50$). If the angle of incidence is $30^\circ$, what is the angle of refraction?

---

### Variables and Units

Before solving the problem, here is a breakdown of the variables used in Snell's Law and their standard units:

*   **$n_1$**: Refractive index of the first medium (where the light originates). It is a dimensionless quantity (has no units) because it is a ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to the speed of light in the medium.
*   **$n_2$**: Refractive index of the second medium (where the light enters). Also dimensionless.
*   **$\theta_1$** (theta one): Angle of incidence. This is the angle between the incoming light ray and the "normal" (a line drawn perpendicular to the boundary between the two mediums). It is measured in **degrees ($^\circ$)** or radians.
*   **$\theta_2$** (theta two): Angle of refraction. This is the angle between the refracted (bent) light ray and the normal line in the second medium. Measured in **degrees ($^\circ$)** or radians.

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Given:**
*   $n_1 \text{ (air)} = 1.00$
*   $n_2 \text{ (glass)} = 1.50$
*   $\theta_1 = 30^\circ$

**Step 1: State Snell's Law**
Snell's Law describes the relationship between the angles of incidence and refraction when light passes through a boundary between two different isotropic media:
$$n_1 \sin(\theta_1) = n_2 \sin(\theta_2)$$

**Step 2: Substitute the known values into the equation**
We plug in our given refractive indices and the angle of incidence:
$$1.00 \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$

**Step 3: Simplify the left side of the equation**
The sine of 30 degrees is exactly 0.5. 
$$1.00 \cdot 0.5 = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$
$$0.5 = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)$$

**Step 4: Isolate $\sin(\theta_2)$**
Divide both sides by 1.50 to get the sine of the angle of refraction by itself:
$$\sin(\theta_2) = \frac{0.5}{1.50}$$
$$\sin(\theta_2) = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333$$

**Step 5: Solve for $\theta_2$**
To find the angle itself, we take the inverse sine (arcsine) of 0.3333. Make sure your calculator is in degree mode!
$$\theta_2 = \arcsin(0.3333)$$
$$\theta_2 \approx 19.47^\circ$$

**Answer:**
The angle of refraction is approximately **$19.47^\circ$**. Because the light is traveling from a less dense medium (air) to a more dense medium (glass), the ray bends *towards* the normal line (from 30 degrees down to 19.47 degrees).
