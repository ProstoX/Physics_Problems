## 5. Inelastic Collision

A 70 kg runner moving at $3 \text{ m/s}$ jumps onto a 140 kg stationary cart. What is the final speed of the cart with the runner? Is kinetic energy conserved in this collision? Explain.

---

### Variable Definitions

Before diving into the solution, here is a breakdown of the variables used in the physics of momentum and energy:

* **$m_1$**: The mass of the first object (the runner). Measured in kilograms (kg).
* **$v_1$**: The initial velocity (speed) of the first object before the collision. Measured in meters per second (m/s).
* **$m_2$**: The mass of the second object (the cart). Measured in kilograms (kg).
* **$v_2$**: The initial velocity of the second object before the collision. Measured in meters per second (m/s).
* **$v_f$**: The final combined velocity of both objects after they stick together. Measured in meters per second (m/s).
* **$p$**: Momentum, representing the quantity of motion ($p = m \cdot v$). Measured in kilogram-meters per second (kg·m/s).
* **$KE$**: Kinetic Energy, the energy of motion. Measured in Joules (J).

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Part 1: Finding the Final Speed

**Goal:** Determine the final speed ($v_f$) of the runner and cart moving together.

**Step 1: Apply the Law of Conservation of Momentum.**
In a closed system with no external forces (like friction), the total momentum before a collision is equal to the total momentum after the collision.
$$p_{initial} = p_{final}$$

**Step 2: Set up the momentum equation for a perfectly inelastic collision.**
Because the runner jumps onto the cart and they move together, this is a "perfectly inelastic" collision. They share a single final mass and a single final velocity.
$$m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = (m_1 + m_2)v_f$$

**Step 3: Substitute the known values into the equation.**
* $m_1$ = **70 kg**
* $v_1$ = **3 m/s**
* $m_2$ = **140 kg**
* $v_2$ = **0 m/s** (the cart is stationary)

$$(70 \cdot 3) + (140 \cdot 0) = (70 + 140)v_f$$

**Step 4: Simplify and solve for $v_f$.**
$$210 + 0 = 210v_f$$
$$210 = 210v_f$$
$$v_f = \frac{210}{210}$$
$$v_f = 1$$

**Answer Part 1:** The final speed of the cart with the runner is **1 m/s**.

---

#### Part 2: Checking Kinetic Energy Conservation

**Goal:** Calculate the total Kinetic Energy before and after the collision to see if it is conserved, and explain the result.

**Step 1: Calculate the initial Kinetic Energy.**
The formula for kinetic energy is $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. The total initial kinetic energy is the sum of the runner's energy and the cart's energy.
$$KE_{initial} = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2$$
$$KE_{initial} = \frac{1}{2}(70)(3)^2 + 0$$
$$KE_{initial} = 35 \cdot 9$$
$$KE_{initial} = 315 \text{ J}$$

**Step 2: Calculate the final Kinetic Energy.**
The final kinetic energy uses the combined mass and the new final velocity we calculated in Part 1.
$$KE_{final} = \frac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)v_f^2$$
$$KE_{final} = \frac{1}{2}(70 + 140)(1)^2$$
$$KE_{final} = \frac{1}{2}(210)(1)$$
$$KE_{final} = 105 \text{ J}$$

**Step 3: Compare and explain.**
* Initial Kinetic Energy = **315 J**
* Final Kinetic Energy = **105 J**

**Answer Part 2:** **No, kinetic energy is not conserved.** In any perfectly inelastic collision (where objects stick together), kinetic energy is always lost. The "lost" energy (which is 210 J in this case) is not destroyed; rather, it is converted into other forms of energy such as sound, thermal energy (heat from impact friction), and the mechanical work done to deform the materials during the impact. However, the total *momentum* of the system remains perfectly conserved.
