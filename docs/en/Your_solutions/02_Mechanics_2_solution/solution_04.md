## 4. Energy & Momentum

A 0.5 kg block slides down a frictionless track from a height of 3.0 m. At the bottom, it collides and sticks to a 1.5 kg block, which is initially at rest. What is the speed of the combined mass just after the collision?

---

### Variable Definitions

Before jumping into the calculations, here is a breakdown of the variables used in this two-part physics problem:

* **$m_1$**: The mass of the sliding block (0.5 kg). Measured in kilograms (kg).
* **$m_2$**: The mass of the stationary block (1.5 kg). Measured in kilograms (kg).
* **$h$**: The initial height of the sliding block. Measured in meters (m).
* **$g$**: The acceleration due to gravity. On Earth, this is approximately 9.81 m/s².
* **$v_1$**: The velocity (speed) of the sliding block at the bottom of the track, just before the collision. Measured in meters per second (m/s).
* **$v_f$**: The final velocity of the combined blocks immediately after they collide and stick together. Measured in meters per second (m/s).
* **$PE$**: Potential Energy. Measured in Joules (J).
* **$KE$**: Kinetic Energy. Measured in Joules (J).
* **$p$**: Momentum, which is the product of mass and velocity. Measured in kilogram-meters per second (kg·m/s).

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

This problem must be solved in two distinct parts. First, we use the **Conservation of Energy** to find out how fast the first block is moving when it reaches the bottom of the track. Second, we use the **Conservation of Momentum** to find the speed of both blocks after they collide.

#### Part 1: Conservation of Energy (Sliding Down the Track)

**Goal:** Find the speed ($v_1$) of the **0.5 kg** block at the bottom of the track.

**Step 1: Apply the Conservation of Energy principle.**
Assuming a frictionless track, the block's initial gravitational Potential Energy ($PE$) at the top is completely converted into Kinetic Energy ($KE$) at the bottom.
$$PE_{initial} = KE_{final}$$

**Step 2: Set up the specific formulas.**
$$m_1gh = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2$$

**Step 3: Simplify and rearrange the equation to solve for $v_1$.**
Because $m_1$ is on both sides of the equation, we can cancel it out. This shows that the speed at the bottom depends only on height and gravity, not the mass of the block.
$$gh = \frac{1}{2}v_1^2$$
$$2gh = v_1^2$$
$$v_1 = \sqrt{2gh}$$

**Step 4: Substitute the known values and calculate $v_1$.**
* $g \approx 9.81$ m/s²
* $h$ = **3.0 m**

$$v_1 = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.0}$$
$$v_1 = \sqrt{58.86}$$
$$v_1 \approx 7.672 \text{ m/s}$$

#### Part 2: Conservation of Momentum (The Collision)

**Goal:** Find the final speed ($v_f$) of the combined blocks after they collide.

**Step 1: Apply the Conservation of Momentum principle.**
In any collision, the total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after the collision. Because the blocks stick together, this is a perfectly inelastic collision.
$$p_{initial} = p_{final}$$

**Step 2: Set up the momentum equation.**
The initial momentum comes entirely from the first block, since the second block is at rest (its velocity is 0). After the collision, the blocks move together as a single combined mass.
$$m_1v_1 + m_2(0) = (m_1 + m_2)v_f$$
$$m_1v_1 = (m_1 + m_2)v_f$$

**Step 3: Rearrange to solve for $v_f$.**
$$v_f = \frac{m_1v_1}{m_1 + m_2}$$

**Step 4: Substitute the known values and calculate $v_f$.**
* $m_1$ = **0.5 kg**
* $m_2$ = **1.5 kg**
* $v_1 \approx 7.672$ m/s

$$v_f = \frac{0.5 \cdot 7.672}{0.5 + 1.5}$$
$$v_f = \frac{3.836}{2.0}$$
$$v_f \approx 1.918$$

**Answer:** The speed of the combined mass just after the collision is approximately **1.92 m/s**.
