## 6. Energy Dissipation

A tennis ball is dropped from a height of $2.0$ m. After each bounce, it loses 30% of its mechanical energy. To what height does it rise after the second bounce?

---

### Variable Definitions

Before proceeding with the calculations, here is a breakdown of the variables used in this problem:

* **$h_0$**: The initial drop height. Measured in meters (m).
* **$h_1$**: The maximum height reached after the first bounce. Measured in meters (m).
* **$h_2$**: The maximum height reached after the second bounce. Measured in meters (m).
* **$PE$**: Gravitational Potential Energy, which is the energy stored based on the object's height. Measured in Joules (J).
* **$m$**: The mass of the tennis ball. Measured in kilograms (kg). *(Note: Mass will cancel out in our proportional calculations).*
* **$g$**: The acceleration due to gravity. Measured in meters per second squared (m/s²).

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Goal:** Find the peak height of the tennis ball after it bounces twice, given it loses **30%** of its mechanical energy on each impact.

**Step 1: Relate mechanical energy to height.**
At the very top of any bounce (just before it falls back down), the ball's velocity is zero, meaning all of its mechanical energy is stored as Gravitational Potential Energy. The formula for potential energy is:
$$PE = mgh$$
Because the mass ($m$) of the ball and gravity ($g$) remain constant, the potential energy is strictly proportional to the height ($h$). This means if the ball loses a certain percentage of its energy, it loses that exact same percentage of its maximum height.

**Step 2: Determine the energy retention factor.**
If the ball loses **30%** of its energy upon hitting the ground, it retains the remaining **70%**. 
To use this in calculations, we convert the percentage to a decimal multiplier: **0.70**.

**Step 3: Calculate the height after the first bounce ($h_1$).**
Multiply the initial height ($h_0 = 2.0$ m) by the energy retention factor.
$$h_1 = 0.70 \cdot h_0$$
$$h_1 = 0.70 \cdot 2.0$$
$$h_1 = 1.4$$
After the first bounce, the ball rises to a height of **1.4 m**.

**Step 4: Calculate the height after the second bounce ($h_2$).**
Apply the energy retention factor again, this time to the height of the first bounce ($h_1$).
$$h_2 = 0.70 \cdot h_1$$
$$h_2 = 0.70 \cdot 1.4$$
$$h_2 = 0.98$$

**Answer:** The tennis ball rises to a height of **0.98 meters** after the second bounce.
