## 9. Current

Charge flowing through the wire is given by the function of time $Q(t) = 5t^2+5$. What is the current at $t=3$ s?

---

### Definitions

* $Q(t) =$ electric charge in **coulombs (C)**
* $I(t) =$ electric current in **amperes (A)**
* $t =$ time in **seconds (s)**

### Solution

We are given the charge function:


$$Q(t) = 5t^2 + 5$$

Current is the rate of change of charge with respect to time:


$$I(t) = \frac{dQ}{dt}$$

#### Step 1: Differentiate the charge function

Using the power rule to differentiate $Q(t)$:


$$\frac{d}{dt}(5t^2 + 5) = 10t + 0$$

So, the current function is:


$$I(t) = 10t$$

#### Step 2: Evaluate at $t = 3$ s

Substitute $t = 3$:


$$I(3) = 10(3)$$

$$I(3) = 30$$

### Answer

$$\boxed{30\text{ A}}$$

### Why this works

Electric current measures how quickly charge changes over time. Since the charge function increases as time passes, taking the derivative gives the **instantaneous current** at any specific moment.
