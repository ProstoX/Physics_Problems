## 13. Transformer Ratio

A transformer is used to step down the voltage from 120 V AC to 9.0 V AC. If the primary coil has 400 turns, how many turns must the secondary coil have?

---

## Solution: Transformer Ratio

### What the symbols mean

* $V_p$ = primary voltage, measured in volts (V)
* $V_s$ = secondary voltage, measured in volts (V)
* $N_p$ = number of turns in the primary coil, unitless
* $N_s$ = number of turns in the secondary coil, unitless

For an ideal transformer, the voltage ratio equals the turns ratio:

$$\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

---

### Given

* Primary voltage: $V_p = 120\text{ V}$
* Secondary voltage: $V_s = 9.0\text{ V}$
* Primary turns: $N_p = 400$

We need to find $N_s$.

### Step 1: Write the transformer ratio and substitute values

$$\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

Substituting the known values:


$$\frac{9.0}{120} = \frac{N_s}{400}$$

### Step 2: Solve for $N_s$

Multiply both sides by 400:


$$N_s = 400 \times \frac{9.0}{120}$$

$$N_s = 400 \times 0.075$$

$$N_s = 30$$

---

### Answer

$$\boxed{30 \text{ turns}}$$

The secondary coil must have **30 turns**.
