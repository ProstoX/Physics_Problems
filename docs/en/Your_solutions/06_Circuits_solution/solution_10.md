## 10. Average Current

A lightning bolt transfers a charge of 30 Coulombs to the ground in a time of 2 milliseconds. What is the average current of the lightning bolt?

---

### Definitions

* $Q =$ electric charge in **coulombs (C)**
* $I =$ electric current in **amperes (A)**
* $t =$ time in **seconds (s)**

### Solution

The formula for average current is:


$$I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}$$

#### Step 1: Identify and convert given values

* $\Delta Q = 30\text{ C}$
* $\Delta t = 2\text{ ms} = 0.002\text{ s}$

#### Step 2: Substitute into the formula

$$I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{30\text{ C}}{0.002\text{ s}}$$

#### Step 3: Calculate

$$I_{\text{avg}} = 15,000\text{ A}$$

### Answer

$$\boxed{15,000\text{ A}} \quad \text{or} \quad \boxed{15\text{ kA}}$$

### Why this works

Current measures how much charge flows per unit time. Because the lightning bolt transfers a large amount of charge in an extremely short time interval, the resulting average current is very high.
