### Step 1: Identify the Initial State

First, we start with the standard formula for the gravitational force. This represents the initial state before any changes are made.

**Initial Force ($F_{old}$):**


$$F_{old} = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$

**Where:**

* **$F$ (Gravitational Force):** Represents the attractive force pulling the two masses toward each other. **Standard Unit:** Newtons (N)

* **$G$ (Gravitational Constant):** The empirical physical constant used in the calculation of gravitational effects. **Constant Value:** $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$


* **$m_1$ (Mass 1):** The mass of the first object. **Standard Unit:** Kilograms (kg)


* **$m_2$ (Mass 2):** The mass of the second object. **Standard Unit:** Kilograms (kg)


* **$r$ (Distance):** The straight-line distance between the exact centers of the two masses. **Standard Unit:** Meters (m)


### Step 2: Apply the Changes

Next, we translate the problem statement into mathematical changes for each variable. We use arrows to show the transformation from the old value to the new value.

* **Distance is doubled:** $r \rightarrow 2r$
* **Mass 1 is halved:** $m_1 \rightarrow \frac{1}{2}m_1$
* **Mass 2 is halved:** $m_2 \rightarrow \frac{1}{2}m_2$

### Step 3: Substitute and Solve

Now, we substitute these new values into the force equation to find the new force ($F_{new}$).

$$F_{new} = G \frac{(\frac{1}{2}m_1) (\frac{1}{2}m_2)}{(2r)^2}$$

Simplify the numerator (masses):


$$\frac{1}{2}m_1 \cdot \frac{1}{2}m_2 \rightarrow \frac{1}{4}m_1m_2$$

Simplify the denominator (distance):


$$(2r)^2 \rightarrow 4r^2$$

Combine the simplified parts back into the equation:


$$F_{new} = G \frac{\frac{1}{4}m_1 m_2}{4r^2}$$

Factor out the numbers to see the relationship to the original force:


$$F_{new} = \left( \frac{\frac{1}{4}}{4} \right) \cdot \left( G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} \right)$$

$$F_{new} = \frac{1}{16} \cdot F_{old}$$

### Answer: The new force is **$\frac{1}{16}$** of the original force.

### Check with Substitution

Here we substitute example values for our variables. We will ignore $G$ (set to 1) for simplicity.

**Initial Values:**
Let $m_1 = 8$, $m_2 = 8$, and $r = 2$.


$$F_{old} = \frac{8 \cdot 8}{2^2} = \frac{64}{4} = \mathbf{16}$$

**New Values (Applying the changes):**

* $m_1$ (halved) $\rightarrow 4$
* $m_2$ (halved) $\rightarrow 4$
* $r$ (doubled) $\rightarrow 4$

**Calculate New Force:**


$$F_{new} = \frac{4 \cdot 4}{4^2} = \frac{16}{16} = \mathbf{1}$$

**Verification:**
Does $16 \times \frac{1}{16} = 1$?


$$16 \div 16 = 1$$


**Check passed.**
