## 1. Wave Properties

A sound wave in air has a frequency of 440 Hz. If the speed of sound in air is 343 m/s, what is its wavelength? What is its wavelength in water, where the speed of sound is 1482 m/s?

---

### **Variables and Units**

Before diving into the math, here is a quick breakdown of the symbols used in the wave equation:

* **$v$ (Velocity/Speed):** The speed at which the wave propagates through a medium. Measured in **meters per second (m/s)**.
* **$f$ (Frequency):** The number of wave cycles that pass a fixed point in one second. Measured in **Hertz (Hz)**, which is equivalent to cycles per second ($1/\text{s}$). 
* **$\lambda$ (Lambda):** The wavelength, which is the physical distance between two consecutive identical points on the wave (like crest to crest). Measured in **meters (m)**.

---

### **Step-by-Step Solution**

**Step 1: Understand the core wave equation**
The relationship between speed, frequency, and wavelength is defined by the wave equation:
$$v = f \cdot \lambda$$

Since we want to find the wavelength ($\lambda$), we need to rearrange the formula by dividing both sides by the frequency ($f$):
$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f}$$

*Note: When a sound wave moves from one medium to another (like from air to water), its frequency ($f$) remains constant. Only the speed ($v$) and the wavelength ($\lambda$) change.*

**Step 2: Calculate the wavelength in air**
We are given the speed of sound in air ($v = 343 \text{ m/s}$) and the frequency ($f = 440 \text{ Hz}$). Plug these values into the rearranged equation:
$$\lambda_{\text{air}} = \frac{343}{440}$$
$$\lambda_{\text{air}} \approx 0.780 \text{ m}$$

**Step 3: Calculate the wavelength in water**
Now, use the speed of sound in water ($v = 1482 \text{ m/s}$) while keeping the frequency the same ($440 \text{ Hz}$):
$$\lambda_{\text{water}} = \frac{1482}{440}$$
$$\lambda_{\text{water}} \approx 3.368 \text{ m}$$
