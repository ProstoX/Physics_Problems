## 7. Standing Wave Modes

A standing wave with four antinodes is produced on a string of length L = 80 cm. What is the wavelength of this wave?

---

### Variables and Units Explained

* $L$: **Length**. The total length of the string. Measured in distance units, typically centimeters (**cm**) or meters (**m**).
* $n$: **Number of antinodes**. The number of vibrating loops (maximum displacement points) on the string. This is also referred to as the harmonic number. It is a simple integer count and has no units (dimensionless).
* $\lambda$ (lambda): **Wavelength**. The physical distance over which the wave's shape repeats (one complete cycle). Measured in distance units, typically centimeters (**cm**) or meters (**m**).

---

### Solution

**Step 1: Understand the relationship between string length and wavelength**
For a standing wave on a string fixed at both ends, the string naturally divides into vibrating segments. Each of these segments, or "loops", represents an antinode. The physical length of one antinode is exactly one-half of a full wavelength ($\frac{\lambda}{2}$). 

Therefore, the total length of the string is equal to the number of antinodes multiplied by a half-wavelength:
$$L = n \cdot \frac{\lambda}{2}$$

**Step 2: Substitute the known values into the equation**
We are given the total string length as **80 cm** and the number of antinodes as **4**.
$$80 = 4 \cdot \frac{\lambda}{2}$$

**Step 3: Solve for the wavelength**
First, simplify the right side of the equation by multiplying $4$ and $\frac{1}{2}$:
$$80 = 2\lambda$$

Next, divide both sides by **2** to isolate the wavelength:
$$\lambda = 40$$

The wavelength of this standing wave is **40 cm**.
