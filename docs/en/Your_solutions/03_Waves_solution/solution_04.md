## 4. Phase Difference

What is the phase difference in radians between two points on a wave that are separated by a distance of $\lambda/3$? 

---

### Problem Breakdown: Variables and Units
Before solving the problem, here is a quick breakdown of the symbols used in the formula and their standard units:

* **$\Delta \phi$ (Delta phi):** Phase difference. This represents the difference in the wave cycle between two points. It is typically measured in **radians (rad)** or degrees ($^\circ$).
* **$\Delta x$ (Delta x):** Path difference. This is the physical distance separating the two points on the wave. It is measured in **meters (m)**.
* **$\lambda$ (Lambda):** Wavelength. This is the physical length of one complete wave cycle. It is measured in **meters (m)**.
* **$\pi$ (Pi):** A mathematical constant representing the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter ($\approx 3.14159$). It is **unitless**.

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Understand the relationship between wavelength and phase.**
A wave repeats its shape over a physical distance of one wavelength ($\lambda$). In terms of phase angle, one complete wave cycle corresponds to a full circle, which is $2\pi$ radians. Therefore, a path difference of $\lambda$ corresponds to a phase difference of $2\pi$ radians. 

We can express this relationship with the following formula:
$$\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x$$

**Step 2: Substitute the given values into the formula.**
The problem states that the two points are separated by a distance of $\lambda/3$. This is our path difference ($\Delta x$).
Substitute $\Delta x = \lambda/3$ into the equation:
$$\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \left( \frac{\lambda}{3} \right)$$

**Step 3: Simplify the expression.**
The $\lambda$ in the numerator and the $\lambda$ in the denominator cancel each other out:
$$\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{3}$$

**Final Answer:**
The phase difference between the two points is **$2\pi/3$ radians**.
