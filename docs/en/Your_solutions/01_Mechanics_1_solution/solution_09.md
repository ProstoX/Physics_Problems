### Variables and Units Glossary

Before we calculate the solution, here is a breakdown of the mathematical symbols used in this problem and their standard SI units:

* **$p$ (Momentum):** The quantity of motion an object possesses, calculated as the product of its mass and velocity. Measured in kilogram-meters per second (kg m/s).
* **$m$ (Mass):** The amount of matter in the object. While often given in grams (g), the standard SI unit used for physics equations is the kilogram (kg).
* **$v$ (Velocity):** The speed of the object in a given direction. Measured in meters per second (m/s).

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Objective:** Determine whether $p_{fly}$ or $p_{ball}$ is greater using the formula $$p = mv$$

#### 1. Convert Masses to Standard SI Units (Kilograms)
To ensure our final momentum units are correct (kg m/s), we must first convert the given masses from grams to kilograms by dividing by 1000.

* **Mass of the fly ($m_f$):** 2 g / 1000 = 0.002 kg
* **Mass of the tennis ball ($m_b$):** 60 g / 1000 = 0.06 kg

#### 2. Calculate the Momentum of the Fly
Multiply the fly's mass in kilograms by its velocity (10 m/s).

$$p_f = m_f v_f$$
$$p_f = 0.002 \times 10 = 0.02 \text{ kg m/s}$$

#### 3. Calculate the Momentum of the Tennis Ball
Multiply the tennis ball's mass in kilograms by its velocity (1 m/s).

$$p_b = m_b v_b$$
$$p_b = 0.06 \times 1 = 0.06 \text{ kg m/s}$$

#### 4. Compare the Results
Now we simply compare the two momentum values:
* Fly's momentum: 0.02 kg m/s
* Ball's momentum: 0.06 kg m/s

Since 0.06 is greater than 0.02, the **tennis ball has greater momentum**. Even though the fly is moving ten times faster, the tennis ball is thirty times more massive, which gives it a larger overall quantity of motion.
