## 5. Field Levitation

What electric field strength is required to make a proton levitate against Earth's gravity at the level of the Earth's surface? (Mass of proton $m_p \approx 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$, charge of proton $e \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$, gravitational acceleration $g \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$).

---

### Variables and Units Explained

Before setting up the math, here is a quick breakdown of the variables used to solve this problem:

*   **$F_g$ (Gravitational Force or Weight):** The downward pull from the Earth. Measured in **Newtons ($\text{N}$)**.
*   **$F_e$ (Electric Force):** The push or pull exerted by the electric field on the charge. Measured in **Newtons ($\text{N}$)**.
*   **$m_p$ (Mass of Proton):** $1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$.
*   **$g$ (Acceleration due to gravity):** $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$.
*   **$e$ (Charge of a Proton):** $1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$.
*   **$E$ (Electric Field Strength):** The intensity of the electric field. Measured in **Newtons per Coulomb ($\text{N/C}$)** or Volts per meter ($\text{V/m}$).

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Understand the Forces at Play**
For the proton to "levitate" (hover perfectly still in mid-air), the net force acting on it must be zero. There are exactly two vertical forces acting on the proton:
1.  **Gravity ($F_g$)** pulling it straight down.
2.  **Electric Force ($F_e$)** pushing it straight up.

Therefore, the magnitude of the upward electric force must exactly equal the magnitude of the downward gravitational force:
$$F_e = F_g$$

**Step 2: Substitute the Force Formulas**
We know the formulas for both of these forces:
*   The force of gravity on a mass is $F_g = m_p \cdot g$
*   The electric force on a charge in an electric field is $F_e = e \cdot E$

Substitute these into our equilibrium equation:
$$e \cdot E = m_p \cdot g$$

**Step 3: Rearrange to Solve for the Electric Field ($E$)**
We are looking for the electric field strength ($E$), so we divide both sides by the charge ($e$):
$$E = \frac{m_p \cdot g}{e}$$

**Step 4: Plug in the Values and Calculate**
Now, substitute the given numbers into the equation:
$$E = \frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27}) \times (9.8)}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}$$
$$E = \frac{16.366 \times 10^{-27}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}$$
$$E \approx 10.23 \times 10^{-8}$$
To put this into proper scientific notation:
$$E \approx 1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C}$$

**Step 5: Determine the Direction**
Force is a vector, and so is the electric field. To levitate, the electric force must push **upward**. Because the proton is a *positive* charge, it experiences electric force in the *exact same direction* as the electric field. Therefore, the electric field must point **upward**. 

*   **Final Answer:** An electric field of exactly **$1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C}$ directed upward** is required to levitate the proton.
