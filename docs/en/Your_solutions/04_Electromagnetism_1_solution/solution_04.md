## 4. Force Comparison

Calculate the magnitude of the electric force and the gravitational force between an electron and a proton in a hydrogen atom (average distance $r \approx 5.3 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}$). What is the ratio $F_e/F_g$?

---

### Variables and Units Explained

Before we do the math, here are the constants and variables we need for both Coulomb's Law (electric force) and Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:

*   **$F_e$ (Electric Force):** Measured in **Newtons ($\text{N}$)**.
*   **$F_g$ (Gravitational Force):** Measured in **Newtons ($\text{N}$)**.
*   **$k$ (Coulomb's Constant):** $8.99 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$.
*   **$G$ (Gravitational Constant):** $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$.
*   **$q_e$ and $q_p$ (Charge):** The fundamental charge of an electron and a proton. Both have a magnitude of $1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$.
*   **$m_e$ (Mass of Electron):** $9.109 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$.
*   **$m_p$ (Mass of Proton):** $1.673 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$.
*   **$r$ (Distance):** The radius of the hydrogen atom, given as $5.3 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}$.

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1: Calculate the Electric Force ($F_e$)**
We use Coulomb's Law to find the electrostatic attraction between the positive proton and the negative electron:
$$F_e = k \frac{|q_e q_p|}{r^2}$$
$$F_e = (8.99 \times 10^9) \frac{(1.602 \times 10^{-19}) \times (1.602 \times 10^{-19})}{(5.3 \times 10^{-11})^2}$$
First, square the terms in the fraction:
$$F_e \approx (8.99 \times 10^9) \frac{2.566 \times 10^{-38}}{2.809 \times 10^{-21}}$$
$$F_e \approx 8.2 \times 10^{-8} \text{ N}$$

**Step 2: Calculate the Gravitational Force ($F_g$)**
Next, we use Newton's Law of Gravitation to find the physical mass attraction between the two particles:
$$F_g = G \frac{m_e m_p}{r^2}$$
$$F_g = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(9.109 \times 10^{-31}) \times (1.673 \times 10^{-27})}{(5.3 \times 10^{-11})^2}$$
Multiply the masses and square the distance:
$$F_g \approx (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{1.524 \times 10^{-57}}{2.809 \times 10^{-21}}$$
$$F_g \approx 3.6 \times 10^{-47} \text{ N}$$

**Step 3: Calculate the Ratio ($F_e / F_g$)**
To see how much stronger the electric force is compared to the gravitational force, we divide $F_e$ by $F_g$:
$$\text{Ratio} = \frac{8.2 \times 10^{-8}}{3.6 \times 10^{-47}}$$
$$\text{Ratio} \approx 2.3 \times 10^{39}$$

*   **Final Answer:** The electric force is roughly $8.2 \times 10^{-8} \text{ N}$, the gravitational force is roughly $3.6 \times 10^{-47} \text{ N}$, and the electric force is approximately **$2.3 \times 10^{39}$ times stronger** than gravity at this scale!
